The Recovery and Resilience Facility (RRF), the bulk of NextGenerationEU, closes in 2026 with no extension. Member States can no longer add measures: all that remains is to submit a final payment request and wait for the European Commission to validate what has been delivered. The question is how much of what was agreed will finally be paid, and what is left of the reforms once the money runs out.

<div class="claves">
<h4>KEY MESSAGES</h4>
<ol>
<li>The RRF pays for milestones and targets met, not for spending incurred. With the implementation deadline now closed, what remains is a technical assessment, not a negotiation.</li>
<li>Spain has received about 76.5% of its plan and expects to submit its last requests at the end of September. Other Member States, such as Denmark and Sweden, have already completed theirs.</li>
<li>The outcome will depend on how each milestone is assessed and on whether the reforms adopted survive the end of the funding.</li>
</ol>
</div>

## What changes in this phase

[Regulation (EU) 2021/241](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2021/241/oj/eng) requires all milestones and targets in national plans to be met by 31 August 2026 and the Commission to make the final payments by 31 December 2026. The Commission Communication [“NextGenerationEU – The road to 2026”](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=celex:52025DC0310) confirms this. Whatever has not been paid by that date is lost through automatic decommitment.

| Date | What happens |
|---|---|
| 31 May 2026 | Last day to propose amendments to the plans; the Commission [could not commit](https://www.eunews.it/en/2026/05/04/the-final-sprint-of-the-recovery-plan-is-underway-from-31-may-no-further-changes-to-the-nrrps/) to assessing later ones in time |
| 31 August | Deadline for meeting milestones and targets |
| 30 September | Deadline for submitting the final payment request |
| 31 December | End of payments |

According to [Eunews](https://www.eunews.it/en/2026/08/31/nextgenerationeu-reaches-its-final-milestone-the-deadline-for-implementing-the-national-plans-expires-today/), since 1 September the Commission no longer accepts new measures or plan amendments when assessing payments. The same outlet puts EU-wide disbursements at €440 billion and the amount still to be paid by year-end at €133 billion.

## Where Spain stands

According to the [Commission](https://spain.representation.ec.europa.eu/noticias-eventos/noticias-0/la-comision-desembolsa-6-230-millones-de-euros-para-espana-en-el-marco-de-nextgenerationeu-2026-08-11_es) (in Spanish), Spain's sixth request was submitted on 3 March and partially approved on 2 July, and €6.23 billion net was disbursed on 11 August. That figure includes €265 million from the fifth request that had been suspended until a milestone on the digitalisation of regional and local bodies was met and new tax documentation was assessed. Three targets in the sixth request are still awaiting clarification. In total, Spain has received €78 billion, 76.5% of the €102 billion in its plan, and has completed around 60% of its milestones.

The [Ministry of Finance](https://www.hacienda.gob.es/es-es/prensa/noticias/paginas/2026/20260811-np-pago-sexto-desembolso-prtr.aspx) (in Spanish) sets out the seventh and final request: 148 milestones and targets, with €21.462 billion in grants and €4.4 billion in loans. First Vice-President and Economy Minister Carlos Cuerpo has [said](https://www.infobae.com/america/agencias/2026/09/02/el-gobierno-espanol-solicitara-a-finales-de-septiembre-el-pago-del-plan-de-recuperacion/) (in Spanish) that the request should be sent by the end of the month. One caveat on the figures: the sum requested (about €25.9 billion) is somewhat higher than what remains after subtracting receipts from the plan total (about €24 billion). I have not been able to reconcile the difference from public sources, so each figure is cited with its origin.

## The case in favour, and the doubts

In favour, the RRF's design has had an effect that traditional funds lacked: making money conditional on specific reforms. An [IMF working paper](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/wp/issues/2026/06/05/the-eus-recovery-and-resilience-facility-experience-and-lessons-for-the-next-multiannual-576636) of June 2026 argues that this results-based conditionality has helped implement reforms in some Member States by strengthening national ownership.

Against, there are three reasonable doubts. The first is timing: the European Court of Auditors warned at the end of 2023 that Member States had requested 16.5% less than forecast (€228 billion against €273 billion) and that 27% of national coordinating bodies considered timely completion unlikely or very unlikely, as [Eunews](https://www.eunews.it/en/2026/05/04/the-final-sprint-of-the-recovery-plan-is-underway-from-31-may-no-further-changes-to-the-nrrps/) reports. The second is quality: meeting a milestone against the clock does not guarantee the intended effect. The third is coordination with other funds: the same IMF paper points to competition between the RRF and cohesion policy as a source of absorption problems in some countries.

## Effects: what happens after 2026

For Spain, the immediate consequence is accounting: each milestone the Commission does not consider fulfilled can lead to a partial payment or to suspension of the corresponding amount, something Article 24(6) of the Regulation expressly provides for. It has already happened with the fifth request and partly with the sixth. The deeper consequence is of a different kind: without the RRF, the public investment financed by these funds (housing, refurbishment, mobility, industrial transformation, digitalisation) will have to be sustained by national budgets or by other EU instruments that will cover only part of it, as the [Commission's own Communication](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=celex:52025DC0310) acknowledges.

The Commission has also announced that it will keep monitoring beyond 2026 that reforms are not reversed once the money has been paid, according to [Eunews](https://www.eunews.it/en/2026/05/04/the-final-sprint-of-the-recovery-plan-is-underway-from-31-may-no-further-changes-to-the-nrrps/). My reading is that this is the real test of the mechanism: not the payment itself, but whether the laws adopted to meet a milestone are applied when there is no money at stake.
